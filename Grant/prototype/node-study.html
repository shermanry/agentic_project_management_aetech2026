Node design study

A visual language for the canvas: nodes that show their content, links that say what they mean, and a layout that stays quiet until you need more. The examples use the Harbor Street Clinic project, and all its data is invented.

Anatomy

Every node has the same four parts

A fixed structure lets people read a hundred nodes without relearning each one. Only the preview changes between kinds.

Content previews

The preview carries the meaning

This is the lesson from FLORA, Weave and TouchDesigner: a node that shows its content is understood at a glance and is more pleasant to look at. A kind decides its preview from what it holds, so a custom kind gets a good preview without any setup.

Decisions

A decision node is a small summary of the decision

It's the most important node on the canvas, so it looks different: wider, with a colored border, and showing the choice instead of a preview. The options, the balance of evidence and who approves are visible without opening it. Opening it leads into the Decisions view.

Semantic zoom

Detail grows as you get closer

Zoomed out, a node is a colored dot and a name, so a project with hundreds of items reads as a map. In the middle you see titles. Close up, you get full previews. This keeps a large canvas both calm and useful, and it carries into the 3D graph, where the far level is the default.

Links

Links say what they mean, quietly

Most links are thin and gray. Their meaning shows in the line style and the end mark, not in loud color. Color appears only where it carries meaning (supports, opposes) or when a path is selected. Suggested links are dashed and ask for a decision; they never pose as fact.

Informs

The default link. A thin line with an arrowhead.

Constrains

A darker line ending in a bar, like a wall. Limits read differently from inputs.

Supports · opposes

Links to an option in a decision. Green means supports; red and dashed means opposes, so it doesn't depend on color alone.

Supersedes

A double line. The older version fades but stays reachable.

Suggested: informs · 0.82 match Try it: keep turns it into a confirmed link.
Suggested by the assistant

Dashed and lighter, with a label giving the relation and how close the match is. It stays a suggestion until someone confirms it, so the record stays trustworthy.

States

One component, eight states

States change the frame of the node, never its contents, so a node stays recognizable while its situation changes.

Overall UI

Calm frame, content in the middle

The frame takes as little room as possible. The rail is icons only, the inspector opens on demand, and the ask bar floats. Three views share one selection: Canvas, Decisions and Graph. The perspective menu sets who the screen is being prepared for.

Decisions view

Each decision gets the layout that suits it

A decision is more than its inputs in one place. Its kind, or a template, picks a layout that suits the choice: a comparison for weighing options, a dashboard for live conditions, a record for what has to hold up later. All three draw on the same data, so a decision can switch layouts without losing anything. The drone dashboard a teammate is designing would be one of these layouts.
